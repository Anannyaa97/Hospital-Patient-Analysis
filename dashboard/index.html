Hospital Patient Record Analysis

Diagnoses, illness severity, cost and length of stay across 382,522 New York State inpatient discharges (SPARCS, 2010).

Overview

Cost and length of stay rise with severity

Both measures shown relative to a Minor case (Minor = 1×) so they fit one scale.

Hover a severity level for the actual values. Discharges with a blank severity are left out of this chart.

Associations in one year of sampled data.

    Data notes

    Worth knowing before reading the numbers.

    • Sample. 382,522 rows sampled from a file of about 2.5 million; counts are sample counts, not statewide totals.
    • Blank severity. Some discharges have no severity recorded and are shown as "Not recorded".
    • Age groups. The five age groups add up to 382,522, which is 10,798 fewer than the sample.

    Diagnosis categories are CCS groupings from the SPARCS file.

    Processing pipeline

    1SourceNY SPARCS 2010, sampled to 382,522 rows
    2HDFS (raw)Hadoop 3.3.6, /healthcare_project/raw
    3PySpark cleaningParse money and stay, drop invalid rows, deduplicate
    4HDFS (clean)Cleaned data written back to HDFS
    5PySpark analysisSeven aggregations on the cleaned data
    6DashboardResults exported as CSV and visualised

    Limitations

    Read the results with these in mind.

    • Sample, not the full file. The roughly 2.5 million-row (983 MB) file was sampled to 382,522 rows.
    • Association, not causation. Severity goes with higher charges and longer stays; this is one year of observational data.
    • Blank severity. Shown separately and excluded from the headline ratios.
    • Length of stay. "120 +" is treated as 120 days, slightly inflating the longest-stay averages.
    • Charges are billed amounts, not what was paid or the hospital's cost.
